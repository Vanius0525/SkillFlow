\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:formulation}

\paragraph{Setting and notation.}
We supply a skill's instructions directly and hold skill selection fixed.
For the same question, the \emph{donor} uses the correct skill and the
\emph{receiver} an unrelated skill of equal token length. Let $\mathcal I$
be the skill's token positions, called the \emph{source span}; tokens
outside it are \emph{readers}. Layers are indexed from zero.
The model processes prompt tokens during \emph{prefill}, then generates
answer tokens during \emph{decoding}. We compare transferring source
states into the receiver with removing source access in the donor
(Figure~\ref{fig:design}).

\paragraph{Residual-state and cache replacement.}
Let $H^L$ be the residual states, the hidden vectors output by block $L$.
Subscripts $D$ and $R$ denote donor and receiver. State replacement,
labelled $S$, writes
\begin{equation}
S:\qquad H_R^L[\mathcal I]\leftarrow H_D^L[\mathcal I].
\label{eq:inject}
\end{equation}
This write occurs during prefill; the receiver then continues normally.
Other positions at that layer are unchanged.

Attention matches a query to keys and combines their associated values.
The key/value (KV) cache stores these vectors for processed tokens.
Let $K^\ell$ and $V^\ell$ be the cached keys and values at layer $\ell$.
Cache replacement, labelled $C$, writes
\begin{equation}
C:\quad
\begin{aligned}
K_R^\ell[\mathcal I]&\leftarrow K_D^\ell[\mathcal I],\\
V_R^\ell[\mathcal I]&\leftarrow V_D^\ell[\mathcal I],
\end{aligned}
\qquad 0\le\ell\le L.
\label{eq:cache}
\end{equation}
The combined intervention $SC$ performs both writes. $S$ alone leaves
these earlier caches unchanged; neither write replays earlier reader
computation. Cache writes precede processing the final prompt token
(Appendix~\ref{app:detail}).

\paragraph{Cumulative attention knockout.}
Let $z^\ell_{ij}$ be the attention score from query position $i$ to key
position $j$, including the causal mask that blocks future tokens.
At every layer $\ell\ge L$, knockout replaces these scores by
\begin{equation}
\widetilde z^\ell_{ij}=
\begin{cases}
-\infty,&j\in\mathcal I,\\
z^\ell_{ij},&j\notin\mathcal I.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:knockout}
\end{equation}
Softmax converts the modified scores into attention weights: blocked
positions receive zero weight and the remaining weights sum to one.
The cut applies to every query and attention head (a parallel attention
component), during both prefill and decoding. It affects reader access
and source-state updates. Path-specific cuts restrict $i$ to readers or
to $\mathcal I$; a reader cut after $SC$ starts at $L+1$.

\paragraph{Recovery and dependence.}
Write $a_D$, $a_R$ and $a_I$ for answer accuracy under the donor,
receiver and a specified intervention $I$. Recovery measures the
fraction of the donor's gain restored:
\begin{equation}
\rho(I)=\frac{a_I-a_R}{a_D-a_R}.
\label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}
Thus $\rho=1$ restores that gain; $\rho>1$ exceeds donor accuracy.
For state replacement at layer $L$, we write $\rho(L)$.
We evaluate \emph{rescued items}, answered incorrectly without a skill
and correctly with it, and retain skill groups whose receiver solves no
sampled item. Screening uses only unmodified baselines, separately by
run, model and task (\S\ref{sec:setup}).

On the knockout's eligible items, let $q(L)$ be the fraction still correct.
Their \emph{source-attention dependence} is
\begin{equation}
d(L)=1-q(L).
\label{eq:dependence}
\end{equation}
It is the fraction of originally correct answers lost under knockout.
Higher values mean stronger dependence on subsequent source attention.
We also compare both curves on common eligible items, retaining their
own baselines and distinct donor/receiver execution backgrounds.

\paragraph{Depth boundaries.}
After establishing effective early transfer, define the first half-effect
boundaries and their separation as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
T&=\min\{L:\rho(L)<0.5\},\\
R&=\min\{L:d(L)\le0.5\},\qquad \Delta=R-T.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:boundaries}
\end{equation}
Layers with $\rho(L)<0.5$ and $d(L)>0.5$ show weak transfer despite
strong dependence. Figure~\ref{fig:transferreading} shades the interval
between the first boundaries and preserves later recrossings.
