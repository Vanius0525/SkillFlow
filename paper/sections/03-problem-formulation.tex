\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:formulation}

\paragraph{Setting and notation.}
We supply a skill's instructions directly and hold skill selection fixed.
For example, the released BMI skill specifies weight in kilograms, height
in metres and division by squared height, alongside conversion rules.
The skill span is this instruction text, not the patient's question.
For the same question, the \emph{donor} uses the correct skill and the
\emph{receiver} an unrelated skill of equal token length. Let $\mathcal I$
be the token positions occupied by the skill text, the \emph{skill span}.
We call positions after this span \emph{post-skill positions}: these include
subsequent prompt tokens (such as the question) and generated reasoning
and answer tokens. These are token roles within each execution, distinct
from the donor and receiver executions. Tokens before the skill cannot
attend to it under causal attention. Layers are indexed from zero.
The model processes prompt tokens during \emph{prefill}, then generates
answer tokens during \emph{decoding}. We compare transferring skill
states into the receiver with removing skill access in the donor
(Figure~\ref{fig:design}).

\paragraph{Residual-state and cache replacement.}
Let $H^L$ be the residual states, the hidden vectors output by block $L$.
Subscripts $D$ and $R$ denote donor and receiver. State replacement,
labelled $S$, writes
\begin{equation}
S:\qquad H_R^L[\mathcal I]\leftarrow H_D^L[\mathcal I].
\label{eq:inject}
\end{equation}
This write occurs during prefill; the receiver then continues normally.
Other positions at that layer are unchanged. The intervention repairs an
execution that has processed an unrelated skill, so its effect can include
both supplying useful information and overcoming that receiver background.

Attention matches a query to keys and combines their associated values.
The key/value (KV) cache stores these vectors for processed tokens.
Let $K^\ell$ and $V^\ell$ be the cached keys and values at layer $\ell$.
Cache replacement, labelled $C$, writes
\begin{equation}
C:\quad
\begin{aligned}
K_R^\ell[\mathcal I]&\leftarrow K_D^\ell[\mathcal I],\\
V_R^\ell[\mathcal I]&\leftarrow V_D^\ell[\mathcal I],
\end{aligned}
\qquad 0\le\ell\le L.
\label{eq:cache}
\end{equation}
The combined intervention $SC$ performs both writes. $S$ alone leaves
these earlier caches unchanged; neither write replays earlier post-skill
computation. Cache writes precede processing the final prompt token
(Appendix~\ref{app:detail}).
$S$ can change later skill states and their subsequently computed caches,
whereas $C$ writes the skill-position caches through $L$. Their combination thus
also changes cache coverage across depth. It is not a controlled comparison
between matched and mismatched histories with coverage held fixed.

\paragraph{Cumulative attention knockout.}
Let $z^\ell_{ij}$ be the attention score from query position $i$ to key
position $j$, including the causal mask that blocks future tokens.
At every layer $\ell\ge L$, knockout replaces these scores by
\begin{equation}
\widetilde z^\ell_{ij}=
\begin{cases}
-\infty,&j\in\mathcal I,\\
z^\ell_{ij},&j\notin\mathcal I.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:knockout}
\end{equation}
Cuts block query-to-key connections; they do not delete skill tokens or
erase information already computed at other positions.
Softmax converts the modified scores into attention weights: blocked
positions receive zero weight and the remaining weights sum to one.
The cut applies to every query and attention head (a parallel attention
component), during both prefill and decoding. It affects access from post-skill tokens
and skill-state updates. Path-specific cuts restrict $i$ to post-skill positions or
to $\mathcal I$; a post-skill-to-skill cut after $SC$ starts at $L+1$.
We reserve \emph{post-skill-to-skill dependence} for these path-specific
tests. The all-query skill knockout alone cannot distinguish blocked reading
from disrupted skill-state updates, or prefill effects from decoding effects.

\paragraph{Recovery and dependence.}
Write $a_D$, $a_R$ and $a_I$ for answer accuracy under the donor,
receiver and a specified intervention $I$. Recovery measures the
fraction of the donor's gain restored:
\begin{equation}
\rho(I)=\frac{a_I-a_R}{a_D-a_R}.
\label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}
Thus $\rho=1$ restores that gain; $\rho>1$ exceeds donor accuracy.
For state replacement at layer $L$, we write $\rho(L)$.
All three accuracies use the same items and the transfer run's own
unpatched baselines. Candidate selection is a separate step: the original
transfer battery starts from \emph{historically rescued} items, selected
by earlier without-skill and with-skill evaluations. Their donor need not
remain correct when re-evaluated in the intervention protocol.
The group screen retains skills whose re-evaluated receiver solves no
sampled item. Section~\ref{sec:setup} distinguishes this population from
freshly baseline-screened knockout and mechanism populations.

On the knockout's eligible items, let $q(L)$ be the fraction still correct.
Their \emph{skill-attention dependence} is
\begin{equation}
d(L)=1-q(L).
\label{eq:dependence}
\end{equation}
It is the fraction of originally correct answers lost under knockout.
Higher values mean stronger dependence on subsequent skill attention.
We also compare both curves on common eligible items, retaining their
own baselines and distinct donor/receiver execution backgrounds.
These are two population-level effects, not complementary probabilities
or the fraction of individual questions that fail both interventions.

\paragraph{Depth boundaries.}
After establishing effective early transfer, define the first half-effect
boundaries and their separation as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
T&=\min\{L:\rho(L)<0.5\},\\
R&=\min\{L:d(L)\le0.5\},\qquad \Delta=R-T.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:boundaries}
\end{equation}
Layers with $\rho(L)<0.5$ and $d(L)>0.5$ show weak transfer despite
strong dependence. Figure~\ref{fig:transferreading} shades the interval
between the first boundaries and preserves later recrossings.
The half-effect thresholds summarize effect size; $T$ is not complete
transfer failure, and $R$ is not the end of skill dependence. Layers are
intervention depths, not successive times in answer generation: every
generated token passes through the layer stack again.
