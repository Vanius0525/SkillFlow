\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the depth gap establishes.}
The paired tests distinguish early effective transfer, an intermediate interval
of weak transfer with continued reading dependence, and later tolerance to
blocking further reading. These operational regimes do not identify separate
internal algorithms. The gap is compatible with dependence on the receiving
state, whose role remains unresolved.

\paragraph{Implications for representation research.}
What a state can transfer and whether its source remains necessary are
different questions. Content and cross-question controls identify useful
procedural information, while position and rank interventions characterise
its usable structure. Failed late transfer and distorted spectral summaries
cannot alone establish that this information has disappeared or been
compressed. Pairing transfer with source-access interventions provides a
behavioural way to distinguish these claims.

\paragraph{Implications for context compression and reuse.}
Representations proposed for reuse should be tested both for recovery in the
intended receiver and for tolerance to removing source access. Our findings
show why token count, state norm and single-format success are insufficient
proxies, and why hidden width alone need not determine a suitable rank budget.
These are evaluation principles, not demonstrated efficiency gains. The
evidence concerns fixed contexts and screened subsets. Layer depth does not
specify agent steps or establish when documents can be dropped during
multi-turn execution.
