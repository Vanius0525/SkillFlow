\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Skill-state transfer is shaped by both the transferred component and the
source access available afterward. Across three models and two task domains,
residual-state transfer weakens before dependence on skill attention
subsides. Content and cross-question controls establish that the effective
early intervention carries structured skill information.

The focused Qwen3-8B/MedCalc study makes this separation more concrete.
Replacing source-position KV caches together with residual states restores
substantial late transfer at layer $16$, while cutting subsequent source
reading removes most of the recovered effect. This identifies source
caches and continued reading as two contributors to successful reuse.
The intervention leaves earlier reader computation unchanged, and the
recutting effect varies with depth, so these results characterize the tested
source components rather than all of the receiver's computational history.

For systems that reuse contextual skills, residual-state recovery,
source-cache compatibility and subsequent source access are therefore
distinct properties to evaluate. Extending the joint interventions to
additional models and interactive agent tasks can establish how broadly
these conditions govern skill reuse.
