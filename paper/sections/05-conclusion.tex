\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Skill-state transfer can fail while the source still supports the model's
original computation. Complementary state replacement and attention knockout
reveal an intermediate depth interval of weak transfer and continued reading
dependence. Its boundary ordering recurs across the evaluated models and
task domains. These operational regimes do not identify separate internal
algorithms: how transplanted content interacts with the receiver's other
states remains unresolved.

The controls make this separation interpretable. Effective early transfer
requires appropriate content and placement, can use information shared across
questions, and depends on multiple leading directions. Repeated late
replacement does not restore early recovery, while unusually large
activations explain an apparent spectral collapse. Neither failed transfer
nor a geometric summary alone establishes that useful content has disappeared
or been compressed.

For context representation and reuse, recovery in the intended receiver and
tolerance to removing source access should be evaluated separately. Token
count, state norm and single-format success are insufficient proxies. The
present evidence concerns fixed contexts and screened subsets; it establishes
evaluation criteria rather than demonstrated efficiency gains. Layer depth
does not specify agent steps or determine when documents can be dropped
during multi-turn execution.
