\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Weak skill-state transfer does not, by itself, show that the original
execution has stopped depending on its skill text. Across six completed main
comparisons, residual transfer crosses below half of the donor's gain
before aggregate skill-attention dependence falls to half. Early controls
validate the transfer probe; common-item and threshold checks delimit the
observed depth ordering. The result remains conditional on the screened
populations and the wrong-skill receiver background.

The focused Qwen3-8B/MedCalc study supplies a partial intervention result.
Adding skill-cache replacement improves late transfer at layer $16$, and
a post-skill-to-skill cut removes most of the recovered effect. This makes skill
caches one concrete contributor to test, while leaving cache coverage,
post-skill-state changes and other omitted components unresolved. The recovery
is incomplete, and the recutting effect varies with depth.

A stronger explanation requires all-layer and late-layer skill-cache
controls, matched alternative-skill caches, and post-skill-only interventions
that separate prompt processing from answer generation. Broader receiver
backgrounds and model families can establish how far the present
separation and partial repair extend to reusable skill systems.
