\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We first establish an effective early transfer probe, then compare its
depth profile with aggregate skill dependence. The final experiment asks
whether replacing skill-position caches omitted by that probe can repair part of
the late deficit. Each step supports a different level of inference.

\subsection{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}
\label{sec:cells}

\paragraph{Tasks and models.}
We pair MedCalc-Bench \citep{khandekar2024medcalc} with skills from
SRA-Bench \citep{su2026srabench}, covering $1{,}100$ questions from $55$
clinical calculators. We also use SRA-Bench's TheoremQA subset, with $747$
questions and $320$ skills. Each question receives its designated skill,
holding skill selection fixed. The main comparison uses four open-weight
models from four families: Qwen3-8B \citep{yang2025qwen3},
Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 \citep{jiang2023mistral}, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct
\citep{grattafiori2024llama3} and DeepSeek-LLM-7B-Chat
\citep{deepseek2024llm}. Qwen3-0.6B provides a supplementary scale check
(Appendix~\ref{app:scale-check}). Models generate
step-by-step answers with greedy decoding, selecting the highest-probability
next token at each step. We use no tools and apply the corresponding
benchmark scorers. On the full MedCalc benchmark, Qwen3-8B
achieves $74.2\%$ accuracy with the correct skill, $34.5\%$ without a skill,
and $31.5\%$ with an unrelated skill.

\paragraph{Selection and evaluation baselines.}
The original MedCalc/Qwen3-8B transfer battery begins with a historical
behavioural screen: $467$ questions from $48$ calculators were incorrect
without a skill and correct with it in that earlier evaluation.
The intervention protocol re-evaluates donor and receiver baselines.
The group-level receiver screen retains $135$ questions from $14$
calculators, without additionally requiring the re-evaluated donor to be
correct on each question. Consequently, Table~\ref{tab:probe} has
$124/135$ correct donor answers, not $135/135$. Its layer-$8$ transfer
recovery is $(110/135-0)/(124/135-0)=110/124$, approximately $0.89$.
Eligibility in the historical screen must not be confused with correctness
under the transfer protocol.

For the depth comparison, we intersect transfer IDs with questions that
are correct with and incorrect without the skill in the knockout run.
This leaves $134$ Qwen3-8B/MedCalc items; transfer retains its own
re-evaluated baselines. The original transfer and knockout protocols use
different attention backends, so common IDs control population membership
without establishing protocol equivalence. The mechanism study instead
starts from $124$ fixed historical IDs and applies fresh baselines under
one split-prefill protocol, retaining $114$ items. Appendix~\ref{app:setup}
details these populations.

The added families use frozen candidate pools of $446$ MedCalc and $255$
TheoremQA questions inherited from the earlier models' eligible transfer
items. Each new model is screened on its own unpatched baselines, with
matched eager-attention protocols for transfer and knockout. One
DeepSeek/MedCalc candidate is excluded in advance because its input cannot
reserve the $900$-token answer budget. These inherited pools and the
wrong-skill group screen define conditional populations, rather than
representative samples of either benchmark.

\paragraph{Implementation checks.}
An identity patch writes the receiver's own states
back and should reproduce its output. Correcting an attention-mask mismatch
between state capture and decoding restores exact agreement, including
all $467$ MedCalc identity checks. All main results use the corrected
implementation. TheoremQA outputs for the added families are regraded
with the official evaluation rules while restoring fresh parser state
before every answer. We retain the original text, verify grading-order
invariance and compare with fresh-process evaluations; eligibility is
recomputed before merging the depth supplements. Appendix~\ref{app:detail} specifies the intervention
protocol, and Appendix~\ref{app:limits} records the correction.

\subsection{Validation of skill-state transfer}
\label{sec:battery}

\paragraph{Early transfer recovery.}
Before comparing transfer across depth, we establish that the intervention
transmits useful behaviour. At layer $8$ on the historically screened MedCalc set,
replacing states at the skill's token positions recovers most of the
correct skill's benefit (Table~\ref{tab:probe}). Patching the last prompt
position or the final $16$ positions has a much smaller effect.
This locates an effective intervention in the skill span. Its larger
number of replaced states also makes it a higher-capacity intervention
than the final-position alternatives.

\paragraph{Content and position controls.}
The unrelated-skill, permuted-position and noise controls in
Table~\ref{tab:probe} all yield little recovery. The noise is scaled to match
the size, measured by Euclidean norm, of the skill-state change. For the content comparison,
the correct and unrelated skills are patched over a common prefix length.
The correct prefix recovers $0.65$ of the available gain. A mean of
same-family skill differences recovers $0.12$ on its smaller eligible set
of $41$ items. Rescaling patched states to match the receiver's norm at
each position preserves $0.87$ recovery, close to the full-span result.
These comparisons identify skill-specific content and its arrangement as
important to early transfer. The group screen matters: other-skill and
noise controls are more effective in excluded groups, so the control
contrast describes the screened population.



\paragraph{Cross-question transfer.}
\label{sec:whose}
In the main prompt order, the skill precedes the question. Causal attention,
which restricts a token to attending to earlier positions, prevents those
skill states from encoding the later question. We additionally place the
skill after the question and obtain donor states from other questions using
the same skill. On $151$ eligible items from $17$ calculators, these states
recover $0.98$ at layer $8$, close to $1.02$ for a donor answering the
current question. At layer $16$, the values separate to $0.29$ and $0.49$.
Early transfer therefore carries information shared across instances, with
greater question sensitivity later. Additional content, dose and rank
diagnostics are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:fullscale}; their role is to
validate the probe rather than supply a separate mechanism for the depth
separation.

\subsection{Weak transfer with persistent skill dependence}
\label{sec:where}
\label{sec:depth}
\label{sec:ladder}

\paragraph{Transfer--dependence separation.}
An effective early probe makes a weak later patch interpretable, but does
not establish that the original run has stopped using the skill.
Figure~\ref{fig:transferreading} therefore pairs both effects at every
layer. On the $134$ common Qwen3-8B/MedCalc items, transfer recovery is
$0.73$ at layer $12$, falls to $0.16$ at $13$, and stays below $0.08$ from
layer $15$ onward. Skill-attention dependence is still $0.93$ at layer
$13$ and $0.75$ at $24$, first falling to at most half at layer $25$.
Throughout layers $13$--$24$, transfer recovers less than half the donor's
gain while knockout loses more than half the originally correct answers.
This is a separation of average intervention effects on common questions,
not a claim that every question fails both interventions.


A stricter paired-outcome check keeps questions answered correctly by both
protocols' donors and incorrectly by the receiver and the knockout
protocol's no-skill baseline. On the resulting $124$ Qwen3-8B/MedCalc
questions, $100/124$ ($80.6\%$) fail both transfer and knockout at layer
$13$, rising to $113/124$ ($91.1\%$) at layer $16$. Thus, the separation
also occurs within individual questions. This diagnostic has a stricter
screen than the main curves and retains their protocol differences
(Appendix~\ref{app:joint-failure}).

The first crossing summarizes each curve. Qwen3-8B/MedCalc dependence
briefly rises above half again at layer $26$ before falling below it from
layer $27$ onward. We retain these fluctuations in the measured curves.
The measured interval summarizes weak transfer with strong aggregate
skill dependence. The first crossing does not mean that transfer has
vanished or that subsequent skill access is no longer needed.

\paragraph{Common-item and threshold checks.}
Original transfer and knockout runs can retain different eligible questions.
We therefore recompute the curves over the intersection of their item IDs,
using eligibility determined from unpatched baselines.
Table~\ref{tab:paireddepth} organizes four models by two benchmarks.
Six model--benchmark cells have verified depth results; Qwen3-8B and
Mistral-7B on TheoremQA remain pending. The skill-dependence boundary
follows the transfer boundary in all six completed main comparisons. For Llama-3.1-8B, $T/R$ is $7/18$ on MedCalc
($112$ items, $20$ skills) and $6/18$ on TheoremQA ($92$ items, $71$
skills). For DeepSeek-LLM-7B, the corresponding boundaries are $6/21$
($25$ items, $13$ skills) and $4/19$ ($54$ items, $45$ skills).
Using transfer thresholds $\tau=0.4$ or $0.6$ and dependence thresholds
$1-\tau$ preserves the ordering in all six completed main comparisons. This extends
the observed separation to four model families, conditional on their
respective baseline screens. The $25$-item DeepSeek/MedCalc result supports
only that selected population. Alongside sample and skill-group counts,
Table~\ref{tab:paireddepth} reports raw boundary differences and their
percentage of model depth, $100\Delta/D$, where $D$ is the number of
layers. This normalization makes layer gaps comparable across model
depths; it is not a benchmark-accuracy ranking. Unavailable model--benchmark
comparisons are explicitly marked Pending. The supplementary Qwen3-0.6B
comparison preserves the same ordering (Appendix~\ref{app:scale-check}).

\paragraph{Broader screening and repeated replacement.}
With the less restrictive item-level receiver screen, $369$ questions from
$45$ calculators yield recovery $0.94$ at layer $8$, $0.79$ at $12$ and
$0.32$ at $13$. The early-to-late decline therefore persists outside the
strict group screen. This transfer-only check does not establish a full
paired knockout curve under the broader screen. Nor does it remove the
wrong-skill background; an equal-length neutral receiver is a separate
control still needed.

Repeated full-span replacement produces the same outputs as a single
write at the window's start in the audited comparison. Under causal
attention and an identical preceding context, later writes can be
redundant. We retain the window results as a consistency check in
Appendix~\ref{app:repeated}, not as an independent exclusion of the
single-write explanation.

\subsection{Partial restoration with skill-cache replacement}
\label{sec:mechanism}

\paragraph{Interventions and evaluation set.}
We compare $S$, $C$ and $SC$ as defined in
Eqs.~\ref{eq:inject}--\ref{eq:cache}, plus $SC$ followed by a post-skill-to-skill cut
from layer $L+1$, denoted $SC+\mathrm{recut}$. The depth separation motivates this test of a skill-position component omitted
by residual replacement. It measures the effects of adding cache writes
and of a subsequent post-skill-to-skill cut while retaining earlier computation at post-skill positions.

We evaluate layers $8,12,16,24$ on a fixed $124$-question set from
$14$ calculators with fresh baselines. Of these, $114$ questions are correct
with the donor and incorrect with both the wrong-skill and no-skill baselines.
Both panels of Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism} use these same questions.
All $48$ own-state controls across four GPU shards reproduce output tokens
exactly. Appendix~\ref{app:mechanism} gives implementation and eligibility
details.



\paragraph{Joint replacement gains.}
At layer $16$, residual replacement answers $4/114$ questions correctly,
cache replacement answers $8/114$, and joint repair answers $48/114$.
Table~\ref{tab:mechanism-contrasts} reports the repair gain.
The joint-minus-additive contrast is positive on the accuracy scale.
It describes the tested conditions, but does not by itself identify a
representation-level interaction or a unique computational mechanism
\citep{vaidyanathan2026mediators}. At layer $24$, joint repair still improves on residual
replacement ($25.4\%$ versus $0.9\%$), compared with $18.4\%$ for cache replacement alone. Adding skill-cache replacement therefore restores part of the deficit.
Even the layer-$16$ joint condition remains $57.9$ percentage points below
the donor's $100\%$ accuracy on these freshly screened items.

\paragraph{Continued reading and attention paths.}
Following joint repair at layer $16$, cutting post-skill-to-skill attention
reduces accuracy to $8/114$. Most of the repaired effect therefore relies
on continued reading. To identify what the original cumulative knockout
disrupts, we separately cut post-skill-to-skill attention, skill-to-skill
attention, or both in the unmodified donor, during prefill and decoding.
At layer $24$, post-skill-only and combined cuts each leave $29/114$ questions
correct, while a skill-to-skill-only cut leaves $110/114$ correct.
Post-skill-to-skill cuts account for most of the tested loss at this layer.
Earlier cuts at layers $8$ and $16$ damage both paths. This decomposition
covers three depths in one model--task setting; it cannot identify the
post-skill-only component of all six main curves. Because these cuts affect
both prefill and decoding, they also do not locate the effect in either
phase separately.

\paragraph{Depth variation and generation limits.}
After joint repair at layer $24$, a post-skill-to-skill recut yields $27.2\%$ accuracy,
compared with $25.4\%$ without the recut. The repaired-yet-reading-dependent
result is therefore supported at layer $16$ and varies across the tested
depths. The main repair gain is stable to generation-length diagnostics:
it remains $38.5$ points on the $96$ questions whose three baselines finish
without truncation. An adverse reassignment of truncated outputs still
leaves a $35.1$-point gain (Appendix~\ref{app:mechanism}).
\paragraph{What the repair does and does not identify.}
$S$ changes a block's skill-position output and can thereby alter later skill
states and caches; $C$ writes skill-position caches at layers $0$ through $L$.
Combining them can increase coverage across the skill-cache stack.
The observed gain is therefore compatible with a cache-coverage account,
as well as with more specific interactions involving states and execution
history. Discriminating these accounts requires all-layer skill-KV-only
and later-layer KV-only controls with existing post-skill caches fixed, plus
matched other-skill cache controls before claiming cache specificity.
These conditions have not been measured here. The present result is a
partial repair and a layer-specific recutting effect, not a complete or
unique explanation of late transfer failure.
