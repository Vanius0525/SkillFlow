\begin{abstract}
Agent skills provide task-specific capabilities by loading instructions and
supporting resources into a language model's context. We investigate what
allows the internal states produced from a skill to support another
execution. Across model depth, we transplant correct-skill residual states,
the hidden vectors passed between model blocks, into a wrong-skill
execution. Separately, we block subsequent attention to the skill in the
original execution. Across three models from two families on MedCalc and
TheoremQA, residual-state transfer weakens before dependence on source access
subsides. Content, position and cross-question controls establish that early
transfer carries structured skill information. To investigate the later
separation, we jointly replace skill states and the source positions'
key/value (KV) cache, which stores attention vectors for previously processed
tokens. On Qwen3-8B/MedCalc, this raises layer-$16$ accuracy from $3.5\%$
with residual replacement alone to $42.1\%$. Cutting subsequent reading of
the repaired source reduces accuracy to $7.0\%$. Skill-state transfer thus
depends on more than the replaced residual vectors: compatible source
caches can restore part of the effect, while continued source access can
remain necessary for the recovered behaviour. The mechanism evidence
covers one model--task setting and identifies source caches and subsequent
reading as concrete factors to test when reusing contextual skills.
\end{abstract}
