\begin{abstract}
When a language model benefits from a procedure supplied in context, can its
internal representation replace the source document, and when does the model
cease to depend on that source? We study this question by pairing two
interventions: transferring states from a correct-skill run into a matched
wrong-skill run, and blocking subsequent attention to the skill in the original
correct-skill run. Their depth profiles reveal an intermediate regime in which
skill states have lost most of their transfer effect but attention to those
states remains important for successful answers. On Qwen3-8B with clinical
calculators, the two half-effect boundaries occur at layers $13$ and $25$ of
$36$. The ordering holds across three models and two task domains. To establish
what is being transferred, we isolate content-specific state differences and
test their position, question dependence and rank. Early replacement across
the skill span recovers $0.89$ of the gain on a screened $135$-item subset,
whereas a final-position patch is strongly answer-format dependent. Recovery
requires appropriate content, placement and multiple leading directions.
Repeated late replacement fails to close the depth gap, and an apparent
spectral collapse is explained by unusually large activations. These results
separate transferability from continued source dependence and provide
behavioural criteria for evaluating context representations and compression.
\end{abstract}
