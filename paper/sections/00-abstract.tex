\begin{abstract}
% Background (1 sentence).
Contextual skills supply reusable instructions to language-model agents,
motivating efforts to transfer the resulting internal representations
across executions.
% Gap / research question (1 sentence).
When such transfer weakens at later layers, does the original execution
also depend less on the skill text, or does the transfer omit components
that still matter?
% Approach (2 sentences).
We pair skill-position residual replacement in a wrong-skill receiver with
cumulative skill-attention knockout in the correct-skill execution,
comparing both interventions on common eligible questions across depth.
Early content and position controls validate the transfer probe, and a
focused experiment adds skill-position key/value cache replacement.
% Results / findings / insight (2 sentences).
Across eight verified model--benchmark comparisons involving four open-weight
models on MedCalc-Bench and TheoremQA, transfer recovery falls below half
of the donor's gain before skill-attention dependence falls to half,
revealing a depth interval with weak transfer but persistent dependence.
On Qwen3-8B/MedCalc at layer $16$, adding skill-cache replacement raises
accuracy from $3.5\%$ to $42.1\%$, while a subsequent post-skill-to-skill cut
reduces it to $7.0\%$; the same layer-$16$ comparison yields a $6.8$--$43.4$-point gain across
all eight settings. This supports partial repair without uniquely
explaining late transfer failure.
\end{abstract}
