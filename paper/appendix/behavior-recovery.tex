\subsection{A sufficient condition for behavioral recovery}
\label{app:behavior-recovery}

For fixed model, positions, last prompt token and mask, let $F_t^D(\mathbf z)$
map initial prefix caches to token-$t$ logits after processing the donor
history $y_{D,<t}$, including all intervening cache updates.
Assume, over the compared states,
\begin{equation}
\|F_t^D(\mathbf z)-F_t^D(\mathbf z')\|_\infty
\le\kappa_t\|\mathbf z-\mathbf z'\|_2.
\label{eq:cache-lipschitz}
\end{equation}
The infinity norm bounds the largest absolute logit change. Let
$m_{D,t}>0$ be the donor's highest logit minus its second-highest.
Margin stability \citep{tsuzuku2018lipschitz} gives
\begin{equation}
[\ \forall t\le N:\ 2\kappa_t\varepsilon_{SC}<m_{D,t}\ ]
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \hat y_{SC}=\hat y_D,
\label{eq:sequence-recovery}
\end{equation}
where $N$ includes the stopping token and $\varepsilon_{SC}$ is defined
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:cache-distance}.
\emph{Proof.} Each logit changes by at most $\kappa_t\varepsilon_{SC}$,
so the donor's winning logit remains ahead by at least
$m_{D,t}-2\kappa_t\varepsilon_{SC}>0$. Induction over the shared greedy
history, including the common stopping rule, proves sequence equality.
\hfill$\square$

This is only sufficient: different sequences may also answer correctly.
Common sensitivity bounds give $SC$ no larger error bound than $S$,
but losing cancellation can increase actual logit errors, so accuracy need
not improve. Sensitivities and itemwise margins remain unverified.
$SC+\mathrm{cut}$ changes the mask and is excluded.
