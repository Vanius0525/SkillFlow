\section{Additional transfer and source-reading analyses}
\label{app:fullscale}

The original MedCalc $\times$ Qwen3-8B intervention battery was developed on the
forty calculators with the most rescued items, sampling four each: $160$ of
$467$ rescued items, and $60$ items over $15$ calculators after the
receiver-never-solves restriction. We reran each of them with every rescued
item of every calculator that has at least two ($467$ items, $48$ calculators),
keeping the intervention configuration fixed. The group screen is stricter
on the larger set because a calculator must fail on up to twenty items
rather than four, so it keeps
$135$ items over $14$ calculators, and we also report the per-item restriction
($369$ items over $45$), which keeps the items whose own receiver fails.



Table~\ref{tab:fullscale} also includes a rank-truncation diagnostic.
We decompose the injected state-difference matrix into ordered directions
using singular value decomposition and retain only the leading $k$.
Increasing $k$ from $32$ to $64$ restores much of the recovery.
A bottom-$k$ control retains the least prominent directions instead.
The formula quarter gives the largest standalone recovery in both samples.
The same-family mean donor changes from $0.05$ to $0.12$, on the $41$
full-rerun items where it is available.

\paragraph{The depth sweep at one-layer resolution.} The subset sweep measured
layers $0,4,8,12,14,15,16,20$ and placed the collapse somewhere after $12$.
At full scale we added $13$ and $17$--$19$:
recovery is $0.98,0.98,0.89,0.74$ at layers $0,4,8,12$, then
$0.16,0.16,0.06,0.06,0.07,0.03,0.02,0.03$ at $13$ through $20$ (per-calc; the
per-item restriction gives $1.00,0.98,0.94,0.79$ then
$0.32,0.34,0.25,0.25,0.23,0.19,0.14,0.17$). Layers $13$ and $14$ are
equal at the reported precision under both restrictions, locating the
largest local change between layers $12$ and $13$.

\paragraph{A continuous readout at the same boundary.}
Accuracy thresholds a generated answer, so an internal change can appear
sharper in this measure. We therefore generate the correct-skill reasoning
once per item and score that fixed sequence under each intervention.
This is teacher forcing: the model receives the recorded preceding tokens
rather than generating a new sequence. The mean token log-probability
measures how strongly it predicts that reasoning. Log-probabilities use
natural logarithms, with units called nats. Higher values indicate that
the recorded sequence is more likely.

We normalize this score as
$z=(\ell_L-\ell_{\mathrm{recv}})/(\ell_{\mathrm{gold}}-\ell_{\mathrm{recv}})$,
where $\ell$ denotes the mean log-probability and the subscripts identify
the patched, receiver and donor conditions. We use $z$ to distinguish this
diagnostic from knockout retention $q$. On $442$ donor-correct items from
$48$ calculators, $z$ is $0.999,0.989,0.960,0.831$ at layers $0,4,8,12$.
It then falls to $0.598$ ($95\%$ interval $[0.550,0.648]$) at layer $13$,
$0.595$ at $14$, $0.340$ at $16$, $0.201$ at $20$, and $0.129$ at $24$.
The layer-$12$ to layer-$13$ decline spans $26.8\%$ of the score's range,
compared with the $61\%$ behavioural decline. The continuous readout
therefore also changes sharply at the accuracy boundary.

The decline is distributed across the four quarters of the reasoning
sequence ($0.20,0.26,0.31,0.21$). Scoring only tokens after the final answer
marker is less useful here. Once the reasoning is supplied, all conditions
predict the final number closely: median log-probabilities lie between
$-0.001$ and $-0.005$, and the donor-minus-receiver denominator has median
$0.0014$, below $0.01$ on $59\%$ of items.

\paragraph{Distributed reading and receiver state.}
An attention head is one of the parallel attention components within a
transformer block. Head-level readout from the skill span concentrates in layers $12$--$21$
after normalisation by residual-stream norm. Blocking the $32$ highest-ranked
heads costs $0.39$ of the skill's effect, versus $0.23$ for random heads at the
same layers (held-out calculators; paired difference $0.154$, $95\%$ CI
$[0.061,0.251]$). Reading is distributed rather than confined to a small
circuit. After late span replacement, downstream readout remains
$0.86$--$0.99$ of the donor's despite weak behavioural recovery. Earlier receiver question-state updates remain unchanged, motivating
the interaction tests in \S\ref{sec:mechanism}.


\subsection{Focused source-history and path-specific experiments}
\label{app:mechanism}

The focused Qwen3-8B/MedCalc experiments fix $124$ historical item IDs from $14$
calculators before inference and partition them as every fourth ID across
four RTX 4090 workers. They use BF16, eager attention, greedy decoding and
$1{,}536$ new tokens. Fresh baselines retain $116$ donor-correct,
receiver-incorrect items, of which $114$ also fail without a skill. Both
experiments use these same $114$ items; all $372$ baseline generations agree
token-for-token between the two suites. The complete repair grid contains
$2{,}356$ generations, the path grid $1{,}488$, and worker identity checks
$100$. All $48$ own-state writebacks are token-exact.

S writes the donor source-span block output at layer $L$. C replaces only
source-position keys and values at layers $0$ through $L$ after prefix
prefill and before recomputing the final prompt token. SC combines them;
SC recutting blocks reader queries to source keys from $L+1$ onward in that
same run. Earlier reader history is unchanged. Reader queries are positions
after the source span; source-self queries are positions within it. The
path experiments cut the specified edges during both prefill and decoding,
preserving the causal mask.

We retain all intervention outcomes, including truncated outputs. For the
prespecified SC-minus-S contrast at layer $16$, the gain is $38.6$ percentage
points, with a skill-cluster bootstrap $95\%$ interval of $[26.6,46.5]$
($10{,}000$ resamples of the $14$ calculators, retaining their items).
The corresponding joint-minus-additive contrast is $31.6$ points
$[14.1,41.6]$; SC-minus-recut is $35.1$ points $[23.2,43.0]$.
On the $96$ items with all three baselines untruncated, SC-minus-S is
$38.5$ points $[25.0,47.6]$.
At layer $16$, S/SC/recut truncate $4/4/3$ of $114$ outputs;
worst-case reassignment of all truncated S and SC outcomes leaves a
$35.1$-point repair advantage. At layer $24$, SC and recut truncate $3/28$
outputs and do not exhibit a reliable recutting loss; this depth is not
used to claim independence after repair.

At layer $24$, reader and source-self cuts retain $29/114$ and $110/114$
correct answers. Their loss difference is $71.1$ points $[53.3,86.5]$.
Reader cuts truncate $21$ outputs; counting all as correct still leaves
retention at $50/114$, below source-self retention $110/114$ (no truncation).
Both contrasts survive worst-case reassignment of truncated outputs.
Analysis artifacts retain arm-wise counts, hashes, clustered intervals
and truncation bounds.
