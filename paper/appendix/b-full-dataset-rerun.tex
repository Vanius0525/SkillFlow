\section{Additional transfer and skill-dependence analyses}
\label{app:fullscale}

The initial Qwen3-8B/MedCalc battery sampled four historically rescued
questions from each of $40$ calculators ($160$ candidates), retaining
$60$ questions from $15$ calculators under the receiver-never-solves
screen. The corrected rerun evaluates all $467$ candidates from $48$
calculators and retains $135$ questions from $14$ calculators. A weaker
item-level receiver screen retains $369$ questions from $45$ calculators.
The group screen becomes stricter as more questions from each calculator
are tested. Table~\ref{tab:fullscale} compares the fixed interventions
under these screens.

For the dose controls, $\delta^L=H_D^L[\mathcal I]-H_R^L[\mathcal I]$ and
the write is $H_R^L[\mathcal I]+\alpha\delta^L$. Rank truncation retains
the leading $k$ singular directions of this difference; bottom-$k$ retains
the least prominent. The formula quarter provides the largest standalone
recovery in both samples. Same-family means use their available-donor
population ($41$ items in the full rerun).

\paragraph{One-layer resolution.}
The initial depth grid could only place the decline after layer $12$.
The completed grid measures every layer. Under the group screen,
recovery falls from $0.74$ at $12$ to $0.16$ at $13$; the item screen gives
$0.79$ and $0.32$. Both screens have their largest local decline at this
same step, while Figure~\ref{fig:transferreading} retains the later
fluctuations rather than imposing monotonicity.

\paragraph{Supplementary model-scale check.}
\label{app:scale-check}
Qwen3-0.6B is retained as a supplementary within-family scale comparison,
rather than a fifth model in the main comparison matrix. Its MedCalc
common-item set contains $90$ questions from $21$ skills. The half-effect
boundaries are $T=11$ and $R=22$, giving $\Delta=11$ layers, or $39.3\%$
of its $28$-layer depth. Transfer/dependence boundary pairs are
$11/21$ at threshold $0.4$ and $11/22$ at both $0.5$ and $0.6$.
On TheoremQA, fresh isolated grading and independent baseline screening
retain $122$ common questions from $92$ skills, with $T/R=11/20$
($\Delta=9$, or $32.1\%$ of model depth). The corresponding threshold
pairs are $11/20$, $11/20$ and $11/21$ at $0.4$, $0.5$ and $0.6$.
Together with the eight main comparisons, these give ten measured
comparisons across five model sizes from four families.
Figure~\ref{fig:scale-depth} shows both supplementary curves.

\paragraph{Same-question joint failures.}
\label{app:joint-failure}
Stored per-skill paired-outcome summaries retain questions with a correct
donor under both protocols, an incorrect transfer receiver and an
incorrect no-skill baseline under knockout. Weighting each group's joint
failure fraction by its item count gives $100/124$ ($80.6\%$) for
Qwen3-8B at its transfer boundary $T=13$, $64/84$ ($76.2\%$) for
Qwen3-0.6B at $T=11$, and $25/43$ ($58.1\%$) for Mistral-7B at $T=7$.
These MedCalc sets contain $14$, $20$ and $15$ skill groups, respectively.
Their stricter donor screen differs from the populations in
Table~\ref{tab:paireddepth}. The diagnostic also retains protocol
mismatch: $4$, $0$ and $4$ items, respectively, are correct under the
transfer protocol's no-skill baseline despite failing that baseline under
knockout. These are paired outcomes under two interventions, not a claim
of identical baseline executions. We report this stricter paired-outcome diagnostic for these three
historical MedCalc settings only; common IDs alone do not establish
that every question fails both interventions elsewhere.

\paragraph{Repeated replacement as a consistency check.}
\label{app:repeated}
On the $135$ historically screened MedCalc items, replacing skill states
over windows $8$--$11$, $12$--$15$, $14$--$19$ and $16$--$35$ recovers
$0.89$, $0.74$, $0.16$ and $0.06$, respectively. Replacement from the first
layer onward recovers $0.98$. The recorded repeated-write and same-start
single-write checks found identical output tokens. With
identical preceding context and causal attention, a full skill-span
write can already determine later skill states, making later writes
redundant. These measurements do not independently rule out inadequate
state maintenance or identify a cause of the late transfer deficit.

\paragraph{A continuous readout at the same boundary.}
Accuracy thresholds a generated answer, so an internal change can appear
sharper in this measure. We therefore generate the correct-skill reasoning
once per item and score that fixed sequence under each intervention.
This is teacher forcing: the model receives the recorded preceding tokens
rather than generating a new sequence. The mean token log-probability
measures how strongly it predicts that reasoning. Log-probabilities use
natural logarithms, with units called nats. Higher values indicate that
the recorded sequence is more likely.

We normalize this score as
$z=(\ell_L-\ell_{\mathrm{recv}})/(\ell_{\mathrm{gold}}-\ell_{\mathrm{recv}})$,
where $\ell$ denotes the mean log-probability and the subscripts identify
the patched, receiver and donor conditions. We use $z$ to distinguish this
diagnostic from knockout retention $q$. On $442$ donor-correct items from
$48$ calculators, $z$ is $0.999,0.989,0.960,0.831$ at layers $0,4,8,12$.
It then falls to $0.598$ at layer $13$,
$0.595$ at $14$, $0.340$ at $16$, $0.201$ at $20$, and $0.129$ at $24$.
The layer-$12$ to layer-$13$ decline spans $26.8\%$ of the score's range,
compared with the $61\%$ behavioural decline. The continuous readout
therefore also changes sharply at the accuracy boundary.

The decline is distributed across the four quarters of the reasoning
sequence ($0.20,0.26,0.31,0.21$). Scoring only tokens after the final answer
marker is less useful here. Once the reasoning is supplied, all conditions
predict the final number closely: median log-probabilities lie between
$-0.001$ and $-0.005$, and the donor-minus-receiver denominator has median
$0.0014$, below $0.01$ on $59\%$ of items.

\paragraph{Distributed reading and receiver state.}
An attention head is one of the parallel attention components within a
transformer block. Head-level readout from the skill span concentrates in layers $12$--$21$
after normalisation by residual-stream norm. Blocking the $32$ highest-ranked
heads costs $0.39$ of the skill's effect, versus $0.23$ for random heads at the
same layers (held-out calculators; paired difference $0.154$). Reading is distributed rather than confined to a small
circuit. After late span replacement, downstream readout remains
$0.86$--$0.99$ of the donor's despite weak behavioural recovery. Earlier receiver question-state updates remain unchanged, motivating
the interaction tests in \S\ref{sec:mechanism}.




\subsection{Focused skill-cache and path-specific experiments}
\label{app:mechanism}

The focused Qwen3-8B/MedCalc experiments fix $124$ historical item IDs from $14$
calculators before inference and partition them as every fourth ID across
four RTX 4090 workers. They use BF16, eager attention, greedy decoding and
$1{,}536$ new tokens. Fresh baselines retain $116$ donor-correct,
receiver-incorrect items, of which $114$ also fail without a skill. Both
experiments use these same $114$ items; all $372$ baseline generations agree
token-for-token between the two suites. The complete repair grid contains
$2{,}356$ generations, the path grid $1{,}488$, and worker identity checks
$100$. All $48$ own-state writebacks are token-exact.

S writes the donor skill-span block output at layer $L$. C replaces only
skill-position keys and values at layers $0$ through $L$ after prefix
prefill and before recomputing the final prompt token. SC combines them;
SC recutting blocks post-skill queries to skill keys from $L+1$ onward in that
same run. Earlier computation at post-skill positions is unchanged. Post-skill queries are positions
after the skill span; within-skill queries are positions within it. The
path experiments cut the specified edges during both prefill and decoding,
preserving the causal mask.

This timing matters for interpretation. State replacement can propagate
into later skill-position caches and later computation at post-skill positions, while the explicit
cache write covers layers $0$ through $L$. The joint intervention changes
both components and their coverage, so its accuracy interaction does not
uniquely identify a residual--history compatibility mechanism. An
all-layer skill-KV-only condition, a later-layer KV-only condition and
matched other-skill cache replacements are needed to distinguish these
possibilities. These controls are not part of the reported grid.

We retain all intervention outcomes, including truncated outputs. For the
prespecified SC-minus-S contrast at layer $16$, the gain is $38.6$ percentage
points.
The corresponding joint-minus-additive contrast is $31.6$ points; SC-minus-recut is $35.1$ points.
On the $96$ items with all three baselines untruncated, SC-minus-S is
$38.5$ points.
At layer $16$, S/SC/recut truncate $4/4/3$ of $114$ outputs;
worst-case reassignment of all truncated S and SC outcomes leaves a
$35.1$-point repair advantage. At layer $24$, SC and recut truncate $3/28$
outputs and show no additional loss in measured accuracy; this depth is not
used to claim independence after repair.

At layer $24$, post-skill and skill-to-skill cuts retain $29/114$ and $110/114$
correct answers. Their loss difference is $71.1$ points.
Reader cuts truncate $21$ outputs; counting all as correct still leaves
retention at $50/114$, below skill-to-skill retention $110/114$ (no truncation).
Both contrasts survive worst-case reassignment of truncated outputs.
Analysis artifacts retain arm-wise counts, hashes and truncation bounds.


\paragraph{Repair extension across families and benchmarks.}
Table~\ref{tab:repair-extension} reports the same fixed layer-$16$
contrast on all eight main combinations. Every row uses its own fresh
split-prefill baselines, retaining donor-correct questions that both the
wrong-skill receiver and no-skill execution answer incorrectly. Repair
and path-cut baseline token sequences match within each model--task
combination. The extension keeps the $1{,}536$-token limit and includes
truncated generations in the accuracy denominator. The control and
mechanism populations differ from the full-prefill depth populations;
for example, Qwen3-8B/TheoremQA has $82$ depth items and $53$ repair items.
The main text's truncation sensitivity bounds apply to the focused
Qwen3-8B/MedCalc study, not automatically to every extension row.
